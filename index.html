What regular stretching does

01

More range

Hips, hamstrings and shoulders reach further. Range grows after two weeks or more of regular stretching.1

02

Feel less stiff

Your back and hips feel less stiff after long days at a desk, in the car or on a plane.

03

Easier everyday moves

Tying your shoes. Reaching a high shelf. Lifting a suitcase onto the rack.

04

Time to switch off

For the whole session, someone else does the work. You lie still and breathe.

1. Konrad et al., Journal of Sport and Health Science, 2024. A review of 77 studies.

How it works

What happens in a session

Every session follows the same four steps. The practitioner guides each stretch and checks your comfort as you go.

  1. 1

    Health check

    Questions about your health, injuries, surgery and medication. Then we ask for your consent. If a doctor should clear you first, we tell you.

  2. 2

    Mobility check

    The practitioner tests how your hips, back and shoulders move today. You tell us where you feel stiff.

  3. 3

    The stretch

    You rate each stretch from 0 to 10. We stay between 2 and 4: a clear stretch that stays comfortable. Say stop, and we stop.

  4. 4

    Two stretches for home

    You leave with two short stretches for the days between sessions. We also suggest when to come back. If every two weeks is enough, we say so.

Who it's for

You don't need to be flexible.

Most people start stiff. We adjust each stretch to your body.

  • At a desk all day

    Hours of sitting can leave your shoulders, back and hips stiff. Tell us where you feel it. We start there.

  • Runners and cyclists

    Calves, hamstrings and hips carry every kilometer. Regular stretching adds range to all three.

  • Sport and training

    Lifting, padel, skiing and athletics all use your hips, back and shoulders. Tell us your sport, and we choose the stretches.

  • Always meaning to stretch

    Stretching is easy to put off. Request a session and lie down. We do the rest.

  • After a long trip

    Long flights, train days and big hikes can leave your hips and back stiff. A session helps them move more easily.

  • Later in life

    Staying mobile matters at every age. We hold each stretch a little longer.

The session

What is assisted stretching?

A practitioner moves your body into a stretch and holds it for you. You lie on a padded table and relax. The practitioner checks your comfort throughout.

What happens in my first session?

We start with the health check. Then we ask what you want to work on. The practitioner checks how your hips, back and shoulders move. Most of the time goes to stretching. At the end, you get two stretches for home, and we suggest when to come back.

What should I wear?

Everyday clothes you can move in. Pants with some stretch work best. Your clothes stay on, and we use no oil. There is nothing to bring.

Does it hurt?

It should not. You rate each stretch from 0 to 10. We work between 2 and 4: a clear stretch that stays comfortable. Say stop at any time, and we stop.

Will I feel sore afterwards?

You may feel slightly sore for up to a day. That is normal. Sharp pain, numbness or tingling needs a doctor. Please tell us too.

How is it different from massage or physiotherapy?

Massage works on the muscle tissue. Assisted stretching moves your joints through their range. Physiotherapy diagnoses and treats injuries, and we do neither. A session can sit alongside massage or physiotherapy.

Why not just stretch at home?

Please do. Stretching at home helps, and we give you two stretches for it. On our table, a practitioner holds each stretch for you, so you can relax completely. Someone also checks your comfort at every step.

Who will stretch me?

A trained practitioner. The practitioner starts with a health check, asks your consent for each area and stops at any warning sign.

Is it for me?

Do I need to be flexible?

No. Feeling stiff is a good reason to come.

I'm over 60. Is it right for me?

Often, yes. We hold each stretch a little longer. The health check decides, at any age.

I have back pain or an injury. Can you help?

We do not treat pain or injuries. If pain is your reason to come, please see a doctor or physiotherapist first. Assisted stretching adds range and eases the feeling of stiffness.

Who needs a doctor first?

Some people need a doctor's clearance or a pause first.

  • An acute injury, or pain as your reason to come: please see a doctor or physiotherapist.
  • A joint replacement, recent surgery or a fracture: we need clearance and any range limits from your doctor.
  • Pregnancy: we do not offer sessions during pregnancy at the moment.

Some other conditions also need clearance. The health check before your first session covers them.

How often should I come?

For general mobility, we suggest a session every one to two weeks. To work on one area, come weekly for four to six weeks. Then we review. Two short stretches at home help in between. If you need us less often, we tell you.

How soon will I notice a difference?

A session can leave you feeling less stiff. More range takes repetition. Studies report gains after two weeks or more of regular stretching (Konrad et al., 2024).

Can I come with my partner or a friend?

Yes. Request a Duo session and stretch side by side.
